# abourazanis.com — Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
The site has to serve three kinds of visitor equally. The homepage should orient each one within seconds and send them to the right section:
- **Hiring managers and recruiters** want proof of seniority quickly: 10+ years of backend and Python work, lead roles, and impact.
- **Potential clients and collaborators** want to know what Anastasis can build for them and how to start a conversation.
- **Fellow engineers** come for projects and, later, writing, and judge the site on craft and clear thinking.

Planned sections: **Home**, **Projects**, **CV**, **Contact**. The structure must be able to take **Writing/Blog** later without a redesign.

### Brand Personality
- **Three words:** bold, modern, energetic, **expressed quietly**. Anastasis rejected a first prototype with huge extra-wide display type, heavy weights and large orange fills as "too bold for my taste". The confidence should come from clarity, spacing and precise typography, not from volume. Keep display weights medium (about 500–560), use the accent for small signals (primary button, dots, section numbers, hover tints), and never use it as a large background fill.
- **Voice:** first person, direct and confident. Use short declarative sentences, concrete claims, and numbers where they exist. Avoid buzzwords and humble-bragging. A little personality is welcome (interests such as airplanes, scale modelling and Lindy Hop), but it stays secondary to the work.
- **Emotional goal:** a visitor should leave thinking "this person is sharp and gets things done", not "this is someone's CV".

- **Minimal above all.** The second pass was still too much. Use regular-weight headlines at modest size (section titles at body size) and no slogan-style hero statements, stat bars or decorative blocks. The portrait stays small (thumbnail-sized), and hobbies get one line.
- **Layout:** somewhere between gthanasis.com and brittanychiang.com. On desktop the page splits in two: the left half stays fixed while scrolling and holds the portrait (about 120–150px, rounded square, softly desaturated), name, role, tagline, a section menu that highlights the active section, and socials. The right half scrolls through About, What I do, Projects and Contact. A single narrow column felt too much like a blog, and the portrait was too small at thumbnail size. Lead with name and role, never a slogan.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme:** dark by default (regardless of the OS setting), with a manual toggle to light that is remembered. Both themes are designed with equal care; neither is an inverted afterthought.
- **Color:** "Ink" neutrals (cool paper in light mode, deep navy `oklch(19.5% 0.03 258)` in dark mode) with **one accent: muted teal** (`oklch(49% 0.08 195)` light, `oklch(78% 0.085 190)` dark). Teal was chosen over ice-blue because ice is too close to the text colour to read as an accent. Orange or terracotta was rejected as looking too LLM-generated, and purple, indigo and neon cyan are out for the same reason. It appears only as small signals: primary buttons, section numbers, the active nav line, link arrows on hover and the contribution calendar. It is never a large fill.
- **Type does the heavy lifting:** a large, expressive display face with a clean, readable body face. A monospace face may appear as a technical accent (labels, metadata, numbers).
- **Energy comes from scale, contrast and rhythm**, such as oversized headlines, numbered sections, strong hierarchy and purposeful motion. Decoration is not the source.
- **References and what to take from each:**
  - [spyroszaf.com](https://spyroszaf.com/): numbered sections (01–04), a problem-first framing of "who I work with", case results shown as big numbers, and a confident two-line headline with a clear primary CTA.
  - [gthanasis.com](https://gthanasis.com/): evidence over adjectives (process steps, production numbers, live GitHub stats), a "Work with me" CTA in the hero, and a contact form that asks about the project.
  - [brittanychiang.com](https://brittanychiang.com/): a text-first hero with one sharp tagline, tight information density, and experience entries with tech tags.
  - [taniarascia.com](https://www.taniarascia.com/): personality and warmth, a content-first structure that grows into a writing hub, and a theme toggle.
- **Anti-references:**
  - Resume template: a single-page CV look with a timeline, an avatar in a circle, skill bars or badges. This is the current site and the thing being escaped.
  - Corporate or agency style: stock photos, buzzword heroes ("Let's build the future together"), and faceless polish.

### Design Principles
1. **Brand first, CV second.** The homepage says who Anastasis is and what he builds. Experience supports that story; it doesn't replace it, and the full CV lives on its own page.
2. **Show, don't claim.** Prefer numbers, outcomes, real projects and process over adjectives. Every claim should be backed by something concrete on the page.
3. **Confident through restraint.** Use one muted accent, medium-weight type, thin hairline rules and generous space. Each section gets a single focal point, and hierarchy comes from size and space rather than weight or colour.
4. **Built to grow.** Layouts and components (project cards, article lists, section headers) must work with 1 item or 30, so Projects and Writing can expand without redesign.
5. **Fast and accessible by default.** Meet WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 text contrast in both themes, visible focus states and full keyboard navigation. All motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`, and pages stay lightweight because performance is part of the brand.

### Current State (from repo)
- Astro static site (branch `astro-redesign`), deployed to GitHub Pages by a GitHub Actions workflow, custom domain via `public/CNAME`. Jekyll and the `sproogen/resume-theme` are gone.
- Tokens and shared pieces live in `src/styles/base.css`, homepage layout in `src/styles/home.css`, content in `src/data/`. Archivo is self-hosted via `@fontsource-variable/archivo`.
- `/cv/` (`src/pages/cv.astro`, `src/styles/cv.css`, content in `src/data/cv.ts`): one bordered sheet on the dot grid, a small pill toolbar (Home, Download PDF via print, Contact, theme), numbered sections, an experience timeline with the current role filled teal. Print CSS forces light and fits A4.
- `/contact/`: same sheet and toolbar (shared `src/styles/page.css`), name/email/message form posting in place to a Cloudflare Worker (`worker/`) with Turnstile (interaction-only), a honeypot and a minimum fill time. Errors keep the typed text; `--danger` is used only for form errors.
- `redesign/index.html` was the single-file prototype the homepage was ported from.

### Content Rules
- **Never show the email address** on the site, because of spam bots. Contact goes through a form (`/contact/`) and LinkedIn.
- The homepage shows live GitHub activity (stats plus a contribution calendar, gthanasis-style) instead of a "What I do" list.
- **Left-column menu:** About, Projects, Contact, then CV last (CV is a separate page, to be built later, so it comes after the in-page sections). GitHub activity stays on the homepage but is not in the menu. Sections are numbered 01 About, 02 On GitHub, 03 Projects, 04 Contact.
- **Phones/tablets:** the menu becomes a bar pinned to the top on a hairline, with a teal underline that slides to the active section. Each section heading pins just below the bar until the next section begins (the gap between sections belongs to the section above). The menu follows the pinned heading.
- **Identity block:** on tablet and desktop, a square photo (about 11–13rem on desktop) spans the height of the name, role and tagline. On phones, a 4.75rem photo sits beside the name and role, with the tagline running full width below. Wrap the tagline with `text-wrap: pretty`. In the one-column layout (below 64rem) the theme toggle sits in the top-right corner beside the name, and a plain centred footer after Contact holds GitHub, LinkedIn and © above a hairline. The footer is a real element in normal flow, not the side column reordered. On desktop the toggle stays in the side footer.
- **Palette:** final choice is Ink plus teal (see Color above). Other navy pairings were tried and set aside: brass, saffron, camel, coral, rose, burgundy, sage, sky, ice and forest green.
- **Motion (subtle, original):**
  - The menu is a hairline rail with one teal marker that glides to the active section, and the active label nudges a few pixels right. On phones the same marker is a horizontal underline in the pinned bar. Don't reuse brittanychiang.com's expanding-line indicator.
  - Elsewhere: sections fade up once on scroll, the GitHub calendar fills in week by week, stats count up, buttons press in slightly, and the theme switch crossfades.
  - Use ease-out-quart only, never bounce. Animate transform and opacity only.
  - Everything is disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Signature interaction:** a fine dot grid behind the page (like engineering paper: 20px spacing, dots in the full `--line` colour). Dots near the cursor take on a soft teal tint (about 45% at most) and barely grow, trailing the pointer gently. This is deliberately not brittanychiang.com's cursor spotlight. It only animates with a mouse and with motion allowed; otherwise the grid is static.
